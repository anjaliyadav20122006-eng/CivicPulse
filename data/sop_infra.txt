MUNICIPAL SOP DOCUMENT: STREETLIGHT, ROAD & WATER SUPPLY MAINTENANCE
Department of Electrical & Roads, Water Supply Board
Document Reference: ELEC-ROAD-WSB-SOP-2024

1. STREETLIGHT COMPLAINTS
- Single non-functional streetlight: Repair within 5 working days.
- Multiple non-functional streetlights on the same stretch (3 or more):
  Classified as a "safety corridor failure" and must be repaired within
  48 hours, since darkness over an extended stretch poses a materially
  higher safety risk (especially for women and children) than an
  isolated outage.
- Complaints explicitly mentioning safety concerns ("unsafe," "women,"
  "children," "night") should be prioritized over routine maintenance
  queue ordering, irrespective of how long the fault has existed.

2. POTHOLE AND ROAD DAMAGE COMPLAINTS
- Potholes reported with the keyword "accident" or "skidding" must be
  inspected within 24 hours and temporarily patched within 72 hours even
  if permanent resurfacing is scheduled later.
- A stretch of road with 3 or more distinct pothole complaints should be
  scheduled for full resurfacing rather than repeated spot-patching,
  which is a recurring cost sink.
- Post-monsoon road damage complaints typically spike; roads with
  recurring monsoon-season pothole complaints in consecutive years
  should be flagged for a monsoon-resilient resurfacing material upgrade.

3. WATER SUPPLY COMPLAINTS
- No-supply complaints: Restore within 24 hours; if pipeline damage is
  the cause, provide a tanker as an interim measure within 6 hours.
- Water contamination/quality complaints (odor, color, taste) are
  treated as Tier 1 public health issues and require water sample
  testing within 24 hours, regardless of complaint volume.
- Irregular supply timing complaints, if recurring across a ward, should
  trigger a pressure/distribution audit of that ward's pipeline network.

4. CROSS-DEPARTMENT NOTE
Wards showing simultaneous recurring complaints across multiple
categories (e.g., waterlogging + potholes + streetlights) in the same
area often indicate a single underlying infrastructure project need
(e.g., a full road-and-drainage redevelopment) rather than independent
fixes, and should be reviewed jointly by all departments before budget
allocation.
